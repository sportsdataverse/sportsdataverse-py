nba_team_luck​

nba_team_luck(seasons: 'int | list[int]', *, league_id: 'str' = '00', season_type: 'str' = 'regular-season', return_as_pandas: 'bool' = False) -> 'pl.DataFrame | pd.DataFrame'

Luck and close-game record: Pythagorean expected wins vs actual wins, and close-game W-L.

One row per team and season. exp_wins is the Pythagorean expectation on the season's final scores, games * pts^x / (pts^x + opp_pts^x), with the exponent x fitted per league on past seasons (~sportsdataverse._common.team_luck.PYTHAG_EXPONENT). luck_wins is wins - exp_wins. A close game is one within 5 points at the start of a possession in the last 5:00 of the 4th quarter or in overtime (stats.nba.com's clutch window; BB-D3's close split).

Parameters

ParameterTypeDefaultDescription
seasonsint | list[int]A season or list of seasons, named by the year it ends (2026 is 2025-26). The WNBA season is its calendar year.
league_idstr'00'"00" NBA or "10" WNBA.
season_typestr'regular-season'"regular-season" (default), "preseason", "playoffs" or "play-in", matched to the schedule's season_type. The exponent was fitted on regular seasons; on a handful of playoff or play-in games exp_wins is only indicative.
return_as_pandasboolFalseReturn a pandas frame instead of polars.

Returns

One row per (season, team_id); team_id is the stats.nba.com team id (Int64). games, wins, losses, pts and opp_pts are from the final scores; win_pct, pythag_win_pct, exp_wins and luck_wins (wins - exp_wins) follow. pbp_games counts the games with published possessions, and close_games, close_wins, close_losses and close_win_pct (null with no close game) are the close games among them. pythag_win_pct, luck_wins and close_win_pct have <m>_rank (1 = highest), <m>_pct (the Weibull percentile) and <m>_n (games, or close games); ranks are within the season, among teams with at least half the most games (close_win_pct also needs possessions for half of them and five close games), and null under five teams. Empty input returns that schema with zero rows.

nba_team_season_rates(seasons: 'int | list[int]', *, league_id: 'str' = '00', season_type: 'str' = 'regular-season', return_as_pandas: 'bool' = False) -> 'pl.DataFrame | pd.DataFrame'

Season team rates from possessions: ORtg/DRtg/net, pace and the four factors, with splits.

One row per team, season and split (all, home, away, close, clutch). Clutch is a possession that starts with 5:00 or less left in the 4th quarter or an overtime, the score within 5 (stats.nba.com's clutch window); a close game is one with a clutch possession. The full definitions are in sportsdataverse._common.team_season_rates.

Parameters

return_as_pandasboolFalseReturn a pandas frame instead of polars.

Returns

One row per (season, team_id, split). team_id is the stats.nba.com team id (Int64). games, poss / opp_poss (offensive / defensive possessions) and pts / opp_pts are the split's totals. The twelve rates are off_rtg, def_rtg, net_rtg (per 100 possessions), pace (possessions per 48 minutes, 40 in the WNBA), the four factors efg_pct, tov_pct, orb_pct, ft_rate and the same four against (opp_*), all fractions. player_play_context​

player_play_context(possessions: 'pl.DataFrame', *, league_non_transition_ppp: 'Optional[float]' = None, apply_ctg_filters: 'bool' = True, return_as_pandas: 'bool' = False) -> 'Union[pl.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]'

Per-player offensive On/Off Play-Context table (CTG's On/Off page, offense half).

For each player: their team's offensive play-context with them on the floor (on_*), without them (off_*), and the on-minus-off difference (diff_*) — which is the number CTG actually displays.

The OFF side is derived by subtraction (team total minus on-court), not by a second scan. That is deliberate: it makes the partition exact by construction — on_poss + off_poss == team_poss and the same for points — so a leak (a double-counted possession, a dropped lineup slot) is impossible to hide. The test suite asserts that identity directly.

Like CTG's on/off, this is a raw split: no luck adjustment, no opponent adjustment, no minutes threshold. It is a descriptive difference, not a causal estimate — for that, use the RAPM surface (~sportsdataverse.nba.nba_rapm.nba_rapm).

Requires off_player_1..5 from ~sportsdataverse.nba.nba_possessions.attach_possession_lineups.
