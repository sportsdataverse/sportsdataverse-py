nba_player_factor_rapm​

nba_player_factor_rapm(seasons: 'int | list[int]', *, league_id: 'str' = '00', season_type: 'str' = 'regular-season', min_poss: 'int | None' = None, return_as_pandas: 'bool' = False) -> 'pl.DataFrame | pd.DataFrame'

Player RAPM split into Dean Oliver's four factors, offense and defense, per player-season.

The definitions are in sportsdataverse._common.factor_rapm: o_efg_rapm + o_tov_rapm + o_orb_rapm + o_ftr_rapm + o_other_rapm is o_rapm and the five d_* components add up to d_rapm, each season fit on its own.

nba_player_game_expected_box​

nba_player_game_expected_box(seasons: 'int | list[int]', *, return_as_pandas: 'bool' = False) -> 'pl.DataFrame | pd.DataFrame'

NBA points, rebounds and assists per player-game against his season, per minute and usage-adjusted.

For every played player-game: <s>_base is his season per-minute rate times the game's minutes; <s>_exp scales that by the game's usage and minutes against his season's (a Poisson model fitted on earlier seasons, scored on later held-out ones). Both use the season without this game (over the regular season (a playoff or play-in game is measured against it)), so a game is never in its own baseline. <s>_exp is null before the model's first held-out season and where a usage is unknown.

nba_player_identity​

nba_player_identity(player_logs: 'pl.DataFrame') -> 'pl.DataFrame'

Human-readable identity for every player in a season's box logs.

Model outputs key on player_id alone, which makes them unusable without a second lookup -- a leaderboard reads 1628983 instead of Shai Gilgeous-Alexander. This derives the display columns from the season's own game logs, so they are season-accurate: a player's team is what he actually played for that year, not his current one (which is what a player directory would give and would silently mislabel every historical season).

A traded player has rows for several teams. team_* is his primary team by minutes -- the one a reader means when they say "his team that season" -- and teams lists every abbreviation he appeared for, in descending minutes, so a trade is visible rather than silently collapsed.

Parameters

ParameterTypeDefaultDescription
player_logsDataFramePer-player-per-game rows from leaguegamelog (the player_or_team="P" variant), carrying player_id, player_name, team_id, team_abbreviation, team_name and min.

Returns

One row per player_id with PLAYER_IDENTITY_SCHEMA. An empty input -- or one missing any required column, min included -- gives the zero-row frame with that schema, so callers can join unconditionally. min is required rather than optional: without it every team totals zero minutes and "primary team" quietly degrades to whichever team_id sorts first, which looks like an answer but is not one. A malformed frame yields the typed zero-row frame instead of raising, so a caller can join unconditionally.

nba_player_war​

nba_player_war(seasons: 'int | list[int]', *, league_id: 'str' = '00', return_as_pandas: 'bool' = False) -> 'pl.DataFrame | pd.DataFrame'

WAR and VORP per player-season, from our BPM, RAPM and BOBAN (regular season).

Each rating is anchored to the team (its players' possession-weighted mean rating over the season is the team's net rating / 5; it is a season constraint, not an estimate for each five-man lineup) and centred on the league, so 0 is a league-average player. vorp is points per 100 team possessions above a replacement player over one team's season of possessions (Basketball-Reference's VORP scale); war is wins above replacement. A replacement-level player has 0, and a season's war adds up to the league's wins minus those of a team of replacement players. rating / war are the mean of the three sources; rating_<s> / war_<s> are each one. The replacement level and points per win are fitted per league (~sportsdataverse._common.player_war.REPLACEMENT_LEVEL, ~sportsdataverse._common.player_war.PTS_PER_WIN).

league_idstr'00'"00" NBA or "10" WNBA.
return_as_pandasboolFalseReturn a pandas frame instead of polars.

Returns

One row per (season, player_id) (stats.nba.com ids, Int64): player_name, team_id (the team he played most possessions for), teams (how many he played for), min, poss (on-court possessions, offensive plus defensive over 2), rating and rating_bpm / rating_rapm / rating_boban (anchored, per 100), vorp, war with war_lo / war_hi (a nominal 90% interval from a bootstrap over each team-season's games: the sampling uncertainty of the team side and the playing time) and war_bpm / war_rapm / war_boban (the source spread), with _rank (1 = most), _pct and _n (possessions) for vorp and war among every player of the season. Sorted by season and war. Empty input returns that schema with zero rows.
