nba_player_impact_context​

nba_player_impact_context(impact: 'pl.DataFrame', *, league_id: 'str' = '00', return_as_pandas: 'bool' = False) -> 'pl.DataFrame | pd.DataFrame'

Append <m>_n, <m>_rank and <m>_pct to a player_impact table.

Ranks are computed within (season, season_type) over the players whose <m>_n reaches the metric's gate (module docstring); a playoff run rarely reaches the regular-season gates, so most playoff ranks are null. A row's rank and percentile are also null when its metric is null or non-finite, when its season or season_type is null, when fewer than ~sportsdataverse._common.league_context.MIN_COHORT_PLAYERS players qualify in the group, or when every qualifying value in the group is equal. Nothing is dropped, and running it on its own output gives the same frame.

nba_player_impact_league_averages​

nba_player_impact_league_averages(impact: 'pl.DataFrame', *, league_id: 'str' = '00', return_as_pandas: 'bool' = False) -> 'pl.DataFrame | pd.DataFrame'

The league-average rows of a player_impact table, one block per season type.

The football F1 rows of ~sportsdataverse._common.league_context.league_averages over the players who pass each metric's sample gate, so n is that metric's qualified cohort: the players with a finite value and enough possessions. It is reported even where nba_player_impact_context leaves the ranks null (fewer than 10 qualifiers, or all equal). category is player_impact_ plus the season_type lowercased with spaces as underscores: player_impact_regular_season and player_impact_playoffs for the releases' two season types. qualifier_min is null (the gates differ by metric).

Parameters

ParameterTypeDefaultDescription
impactDataFrameA player_impact table, as nba_player_impact_context takes.
league_idstr'00'"00" NBA or "10" WNBA: the level label and the sample gates.
return_as_pandasboolFalseReturn a pandas.DataFrame instead of polars.

Returns

One row per (season, season type, metric) in ~sportsdataverse._common.league_context.LEAGUE_AVERAGES_SCHEMA; an empty input returns that schema with zero rows.

col_nametypedescription
seasonInt64Season of the league-average row, as the END year the player_impact table stamps (2026 = 2025-26).

nba_reml_rapm(possessions: 'pl.DataFrame', prior: 'dict[int, tuple[float, float]]', *, penalty: 'str' = 'fixed', lambdas: 'tuple[float, float] | None' = None, league_id: 'str | None' = None, return_as_pandas: 'bool' = False) -> 'pl.DataFrame | pd.DataFrame'

Season impact rating: RAPM shrunk toward a box prior, at a fixed league penalty or one chosen by REML.

The posterior mean of a prior-informed RAPM (the same design and sign convention as ~sportsdataverse.nba.nba_rapm.nba_rapm and ~sportsdataverse.nba.nba_adj_rapm.nba_adj_rapm). The prior is used as given (no scale multiplier). Two penalty rules:

  • penalty="fixed" (the default): the league's adjusted-RAPM penalty, ~sportsdataverse.nba.nba_adj_rapm.ADJ_RAPM_PENALTY (NBA 3,728), on offense and defense.
  • penalty="reml": one offense and one defense penalty chosen per call by restricted maximum likelihood (fit_reml_prior_ridge). REML's penalty is the ratio of the per-possession noise to how far players sit from their prior; with the v2 box prior it puts 90% of the weight on the prior on offense and 81% on defense (possession-weighted), and offense reaches the upper bound in 2018, 2019 and 2023.

Why fixed is the default: measured by tools/fit_box_prior_v2.py on NBA regular seasons 1998-2026 with the v2 box prior, against today's ~sportsdataverse.nba.nba_adj_rapm.nba_adj_rapm on the same held-out possessions, the fixed rule lowers the error by 1.64 bp of MSE (SE 0.10, better in all 29 seasons) and by 1.43 bp after a mid-season cut date (2006-2026, SE 0.29, 17 of 21); REML by 0.73 (SE 0.17, 22 of 29) and 1.03 (SE 0.49, 14 of 21). Head to head the fixed rule beats the REML pick by 0.91 bp on held-out games (SE 0.14, 27 of 29 seasons). The 3,728 was cross-validated on these seasons for the previous-season SPM prior, so refitting it for the v2 prior is open. The cut-date test counts 2006+ only (1998-2005 priors learn from later seasons); height and position come from one later index.

Built for the box prior of sportsdataverse.nba.nba_box_prior (o_prior / d_prior of ~sportsdataverse.nba.nba_box_prior.apply_box_prior), but any per-100 (o, d) prior works.

Opponent-adjusted team ratings (AdjOffRtg/AdjDefRtg/AdjNet/AdjPace), as-of-date aware.

Loads schedule + team box score for seasons, optionally filters to games strictly before as_of_date (the leakage boundary, via ~sportsdataverse.nba.nba_prediction_constants.as_of_ratings_split), computes per-game efficiency, runs the opponent-adjustment fixed points, and adds a per-season dense rank (on adj_net_rtg descending) and adj_net_z (z-score of adj_net_rtg).

Parameters

ParameterTypeDefaultDescription
seasonsUnion[int, list[int]]A season (e.g. 2024) or list of seasons.
league_idstr'00'"00" NBA / "10" WNBA / "20" G-League.
as_of_dateUnion[date, None]NoneIf given, only games with date < as_of_date are used (predictive/backtest usage); None computes full-season descriptive ratings.
return_as_pandasboolFalseReturn a pandas frame instead of polars.

Returns

One row per (season, team_id): season, team_id, adj_off_rtg, adj_def_rtg, adj_net_rtg, adj_pace, raw_off_rtg, raw_def_rtg, raw_pace, games, rank, adj_net_z, then <m>_rank, <m>_pct and <m>_n for each of the seven ratings (BB-D2, the football F5/F6 grammar of sportsdataverse._common.league_context). _rank is 1 for the best team in the season: the highest offensive and net ratings, the lowest *_def_rtg, the fastest pace. _pct is the Weibull percentile. Both are null for a team under half the season's most games, and for every team when fewer than five qualifying teams have the rating or they all share one value. On a full season the half-games rule drops the All-Star teams; with as_of_date the most games is taken before the cut, so an early cut can also null regular teams. _n counts the sample: possessions (rounded) for the ratings, games for the paces. Empty input returns that schema with zero rows.

nba_war​

nba_war(ratings: 'pl.DataFrame', poss: 'pl.DataFrame', *, replacement_level: 'float', pts_per_win: 'float', rating_col: 'str' = 'rating', poss_col: 'str' = 'poss', return_as_pandas: 'bool' = False) -> 'Union[pl.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]'

Points-above-replacement -> wins for each player.

war_i = (rating_i - replacement_level) * poss_i / 100 / pts_per_win.

poss_i counts each possession once, as a team's net rating per 100 does: a game is about 100 possessions a side, not 200. A net rating per 100 (rapm = o_rapm + d_rapm) spends o_rapm over the offensive possessions and d_rapm over the defensive ones, so its points are about rapm * (off_poss + def_poss) / 2 / 100: pass (off_poss + def_poss) / 2, never the sum. The sum doubles every WAR. The 2025-26 NBA player_impact release was built with the sum: its league WAR totals 1,582 against the 791 the halved possessions give, and a team's summed WAR rose 1.55 per win (0.78 halved, under 1 as a shrunken rating's should).
